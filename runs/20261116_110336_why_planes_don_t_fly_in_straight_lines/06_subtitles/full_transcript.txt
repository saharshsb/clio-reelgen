Why do airplanes take giant curved detours instead of flying straight across the map?

That straight dashed line looks shortest, right? On a flat screen, it actually wastes thousands of miles.

The culprit is flat maps. Stretching a round globe onto a rectangle wildly inflates the northern regions.

Earth is a sphere, so the true shortest path is always an arc called a great circle.

Stretch a string tight across a globe, and it naturally curves north, matching the plane's exact route.

Flying that curved arc from New York to Tokyo saves nearly five hundred miles and hours of fuel.

Add fast-moving jet stream winds, and curving becomes the fastest, cheapest highway in the sky.
